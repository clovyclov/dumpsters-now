## Attic Cleanouts in Plymouth Homes

Attics present a different problem: everything has to come downstairs before it can be discarded.

Start near the attic access and establish a clear route through the house. Avoid stacking discarded material on stairs or in hallways where it creates a trip hazard.

Attic contents are often relatively light but bulky, making **volume** the main concern. Boxes of decorations, old furniture, empty totes, luggage, and decades of stored household goods can occupy a surprising amount of dumpster space.

Whenever practical, break down cardboard and safely disassemble bulky disposable furniture to reduce wasted airspace inside the container.

If your attic project involves removing **insulation or other specialized building materials**, tell us what you're disposing of before booking. Disposal requirements aren't the same for every material.

## Basement Cleanup: Pay Attention to Weight

Basement cleanouts often combine household junk with much heavier materials.

An old sofa may be bulky without weighing an extraordinary amount. By comparison, concrete, dirt, brick, tile, plaster, and other dense renovation debris can generate thousands of pounds before a large dumpster looks visually full.

A simple weight formula is:

**Estimated disposal weight = number of units × average weight per unit**

Roofing provides a useful illustration of why material matters. If a square of 3-tab shingles weighs approximately **230 pounds**, then 20 squares would be:

**20 × 230 lbs = 4,600 lbs, or about 2.3 tons**

The same principle applies to basement renovation debris: don't choose a dumpster exclusively by how much empty space you see.

Tell us when a basement cleanup includes **concrete, masonry, dirt, roofing, plaster, or other especially dense material** so we can recommend the proper loading approach.

## How to Load a Dumpster Efficiently During an Estate Cleanout

Loading technique can determine whether you get full value from the container.

Begin by placing flat and sturdy objects along the bottom when practical. Break down disposable furniture safely instead of leaving large empty cavities underneath it. Flatten cardboard, distribute material throughout the container, and avoid building one large pile at the door.

Most importantly, **do not load debris above the dumpster's permitted fill line**. Material must remain safely contained for transportation.

Also think about what you might need later. Don't throw important paperwork, family photographs, jewelry, legal records, medication, or potentially valuable objects straight into the debris stream during a fast estate cleanup. Sorting first usually saves trouble later.
