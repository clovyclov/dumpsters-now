## How Much Does a Roof Tear-Off Weigh? Asphalt Shingle Weight Per Square

The most useful number for calculating roofing debris is the weight of one roofing square.

In roofing terminology:

**1 roofing square = 100 square feet of roof surface.**

A house with 2,000 square feet of actual roof surface has approximately 20 roofing squares.

However, roof surface area is not necessarily the same as the home's interior square footage.

A 2,000-square-foot two-story house might have substantially less than 2,000 square feet of roof surface, while a sprawling ranch could have significantly more.

Roof pitch, attached garages, dormers, and overhangs also affect total roof area.

### Asphalt Shingle Weight by Material Type

Different roofing materials produce different disposal weights.

| Roofing Material | Estimated Weight Per Square | Weight for 20 Squares |
|---|---:|---:|
| Standard 3-tab asphalt shingles | 230 lbs | 4,600 lbs |
| Lightweight architectural shingles | 250 lbs | 5,000 lbs |
| Standard architectural shingles | 300 lbs | 6,000 lbs |
| Heavy architectural shingles | 350 lbs | 7,000 lbs |
| Premium laminated shingles | 400 lbs | 8,000 lbs |

These are planning estimates. Actual shingle weights vary by manufacturer, product line, age, moisture content, and installation.

**Important:** These figures estimate shingle weight only. They do not automatically account for roofing nails, underlayment, flashing, damaged decking, or additional roofing layers.

When estimating a complete roof tear-off, we recommend adding a reasonable debris allowance to avoid underestimating the final load.

### The Roofing Dumpster Weight Formula

Here's a practical formula for estimating asphalt shingle disposal weight:

**Total Shingle Weight = Roofing Squares × Weight Per Square × Number of Layers**

Then:

**Estimated Total Debris Weight = Shingle Weight + Additional Tear-Off Materials**

To convert pounds into tons:

**Total Pounds ÷ 2,000 = Total Tons**

For example, consider a Northville home with:

- 24 roofing squares
- Architectural shingles weighing 300 pounds per square
- One existing shingle layer
- An estimated 600 pounds of additional roofing debris

The calculation is:

**24 × 300 = 7,200 pounds of shingles**

Add the additional materials:

**7,200 + 600 = 7,800 pounds**

Convert to tons:

**7,800 ÷ 2,000 = 3.9 tons**

This project produces approximately **3.9 tons of roofing debris**.

That number matters because dumpster rentals typically include a specified disposal weight allowance.

A container with a 3-ton allowance would not cover the full estimated weight without additional disposal charges.

A 30-yard dumpster with a 4-ton included allowance would be a more comfortable starting point, assuming the materials fit and the actual debris weight remains within the estimate.

## How to Avoid Roofing Dumpster Overweight Charges

Overweight charges are among the most preventable expenses associated with roofing disposal.

The best way to avoid surprises is to estimate the debris weight before scheduling the container.

### 1. Calculate Actual Roofing Squares

Ask your roofing contractor for the measured number of roofing squares.

Do not rely solely on the home's advertised square footage.

A roof's total surface area depends on its footprint, pitch, overhangs, and design.

### 2. Identify the Existing Shingle Type

Determine whether the tear-off includes:

- Standard 3-tab shingles
- Architectural shingles
- Premium laminated shingles
- Multiple roofing layers

Heavier shingles require a larger weight allowance.

### 3. Account for Additional Materials

Underlayment, flashing, roof vents, nails, and damaged decking all add weight.

If plywood replacement is expected, include those materials in the estimate.

### 4. Keep Water Out When Practical

Wet roofing debris weighs more than dry roofing debris.

Rain-soaked shingles, wood, and underlayment can increase the disposal weight.

Don't allow debris to sit unnecessarily exposed to water before pickup.

### 5. Never Load Above the Fill Line

A dumpster that appears to have extra space may already be at its safe weight limit.

Keep materials below the designated fill line and distribute the load evenly.

Avoid concentrating extremely heavy debris at one end.

### 6. Don't Use Volume as Your Only Guide

This is the biggest mistake people make with roofing dumpsters.

A 30-yard dumpster has more physical space than a 20-yard dumpster, but dense asphalt shingles can reach transportation weight limits before filling the container.

**For roofing projects, calculate weight first and choose volume second.**
