## Quick Answer: What Size Dumpster Do You Need for Roofing in Novi?

For asphalt roofing, start with the number of roofing squares being removed and the weight of each square.

A practical baseline for **3-tab asphalt shingles is approximately 230 pounds per roofing square**.

One roofing square equals **100 square feet of roof surface**.

The basic formula is:

**Number of roofing squares × pounds per square = estimated shingle weight**

Then divide by 2,000 to convert pounds to tons.

For example, a 20-square roof with 3-tab shingles produces approximately:

**20 × 230 lbs = 4,600 lbs = 2.3 tons**

That calculation represents the shingles themselves. Your actual tear-off load can be heavier once you include underlayment, nails, flashing, ridge material, starter shingles, damaged decking, and other roofing debris.

## Understanding a "Square" of Roofing

Roofers don't normally describe a roof simply by the home's floor area. Roofing material is calculated in **squares**.

**1 roofing square = 100 square feet of roof surface.**

A 2,000-square-foot house does not necessarily have a 20-square roof. Roof pitch, garage sections, porches, dormers, overhangs, and other architectural features can increase the actual roof surface significantly.

If a Novi homeowner tells us they have a "2,000-square-foot roof," that's different from saying a roofer measured the project at **20 roofing squares**.

For dumpster planning, use the roofing contractor's measured number of squares whenever possible.

## Don't Forget Multiple Layers of Shingles

One of the easiest ways to underestimate roofing debris is to calculate the roof area but overlook the number of existing layers.

Suppose a roofer near **Beck Road and 10 Mile Road** measures a roof at 20 squares.

With one layer of 3-tab shingles:

**20 squares × 230 lbs = 4,600 lbs**

That's approximately **2.3 tons of shingles**.

If there are two comparable layers:

**20 × 230 × 2 = 9,200 lbs**

That's approximately **4.6 tons before accounting for other tear-off material**.

The roof hasn't become physically larger, but the disposal weight has essentially doubled.

Before reserving your Novi roofing dumpster, determine:

- The measured number of roofing squares
- Whether the shingles are 3-tab, architectural, or another material
- How many existing roofing layers will be removed
- Whether plywood or OSB roof decking will also be replaced
- Whether gutters, flashing, underlayment, and other debris are going into the container

This information produces a much more useful dumpster recommendation than home square footage alone.

## Why Roofing Dumpsters Often Look "Underfilled"

A common misconception is that customers should be able to fill every dumpster all the way to the top.

That isn't necessarily true with shingles.

Consider 30 squares of 3-tab shingles:

**30 × 230 = 6,900 pounds**, or about **3.45 tons**.

Those shingles can occupy much less physical space than 30 cubic yards while still creating a very heavy load.

This is why a roofing dumpster that appears half-full isn't necessarily being underutilized. **Cubic-yard capacity and weight capacity measure two different things.**

For shingles, concrete, dirt, and other dense materials, weight often becomes the controlling factor.
