### Understanding Dumpster Capacity

Dumpster sizes are measured in cubic yards.

One cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet.

A 15-yard dumpster provides approximately:

15 × 27 = **405 cubic feet of container volume**.

A 20-yard dumpster provides:

20 × 27 = **540 cubic feet**.

A 30-yard dumpster provides:

30 × 27 = **810 cubic feet**.

A 40-yard dumpster provides:

40 × 27 = **1,080 cubic feet**.

These figures describe nominal container volume, not the amount of debris that can be loaded above the rim.

All materials must remain within the container's permitted fill level.

For household junk, the available volume is often more important than the weight allowance.

However, heavy materials such as plaster, tile, bricks, and concrete can reach weight limits quickly.

## How to Estimate Dumpster Weight for a Moving Cleanout

Dumpster volume tells you how much space you have.

Weight tells you how much material you can dispose of within the included tonnage.

Understanding both helps you avoid unexpected overage charges.

### Converting Tons to Pounds

One US ton equals 2,000 pounds.

That means:

| Dumpster Size | Included Tons | Included Pounds |
|---|---:|---:|
| 15-Yard | 2 tons | 4,000 lbs |
| 20-Yard | 3 tons | 6,000 lbs |
| 30-Yard | 4 tons | 8,000 lbs |
| 40-Yard | 5 tons | 10,000 lbs |

### Example: Plymouth Basement and Garage Cleanout

Suppose you're disposing of:

- Old furniture: 900 lbs
- Broken shelving: 350 lbs
- Household junk: 1,200 lbs
- Cardboard and miscellaneous debris: 250 lbs
- Old carpeting and padding: 500 lbs

Estimated total:

900 + 350 + 1,200 + 250 + 500 = **3,200 pounds**

Convert that to tons:

3,200 ÷ 2,000 = **1.6 tons**

In this example, the estimated weight falls within the 2-ton allowance of a 15-yard dumpster.

However, the material still needs to fit inside the container.

If the furniture is bulky and cannot be safely broken down, a 20-yard dumpster may be more appropriate.

These weights are illustrative estimates. Actual furniture and debris weights vary considerably.

### What About Heavy Renovation Materials?

Sometimes moving cleanouts overlap with renovation work.

For example, a homeowner preparing to sell might remove old flooring, cabinets, or roofing materials.

Heavy debris needs additional planning.

As a rough estimating benchmark, **one roofing square of three-tab asphalt shingles can weigh approximately 230 pounds**, depending on the product and layers.

Twenty squares would therefore weigh approximately:

20 × 230 = **4,600 pounds**

That material alone exceeds the included 2-ton allowance of a 15-yard dumpster.

Concrete, dirt, brick, and other dense debris may require special containers or loading restrictions.

Always discuss these materials with us before booking.

## How to Load a Moving Dumpster Efficiently

The way you load your dumpster can make a significant difference in how much material fits.

Poor loading often wastes valuable space.

For Plymouth moving cleanouts, I recommend the following approach.

### Step 1: Start With Large, Flat Items

Load flat or bulky items first when practical.

Examples include:

- Broken-down shelving
- Flattened cardboard
- Disassembled furniture
- Cabinet panels
- Wooden boards

Place materials carefully to avoid unstable stacks and unnecessary gaps.

### Step 2: Break Down Furniture When Safe

An assembled desk or bookshelf can occupy a surprising amount of space.

Removing legs, shelves, and panels may make loading easier.

Use appropriate tools and protective equipment.

Avoid dismantling anything containing hazardous materials or components that require specialized disposal.

### Step 3: Fill Gaps With Smaller Household Junk

Once larger items are positioned, use smaller debris to fill remaining spaces.

This helps maximize container capacity.

Do not climb into the dumpster to compact debris, and avoid unstable piles that could shift or fall.

### Step 4: Keep Heavy Materials Low

Heavy objects should be distributed evenly and positioned low in the container.

This improves load stability and helps prevent concentrated weight from creating problems.

### Step 5: Never Load Above the Rim

Roll-off dumpsters must be transported safely.

Materials extending above the container's permitted fill line may prevent pickup until the load is corrected.

Avoid overloading one end of the dumpster or allowing debris to protrude from the sides.
